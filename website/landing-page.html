Why Oman-specific

Generic apps don't understand Omani waters

BAHHAR is architected from the ground up for the Arabian Sea and Gulf of Oman — not a global model with a flag pasted on.

Local thermoclines

Summer upwelling in Dhofar creates temperature layers that drive baitfish — a pattern global models simply ignore. We model the Omani water column.

Regional species

Kingfish (الكنعد) and Hammour (الهامور) behave differently in the Arabian Sea than in the Red Sea. Our forecasts are trained on Omani ecosystems.

Legal compliance

Protected areas like the Daymaniyat Islands require permits. BAHHAR geofences boundaries and surfaces the relevant royal decree before you cast.

The coast, north to south

Six governorates, six different fisheries

BAHHAR models all 3,165 km of it, but the coast is not one place — where the water deepens against the mountains, where the protected reserves sit, and what each stretch is actually fished for.

01

Musandam

مسندم · the northern tip, on the Strait of Hormuz

The mountains do not ease into the sea here; they keep going underneath it. Musandam's inlets are steep and deep enough to be called fjords, and the water that anglers want sits right against the shore — so the run out from Khasab to real depth is minutes, not the hour of transit a flatter coast charges for it.

In this water Kingfish · Hammour

02

Al Batinah

شمال الباطنة وجنوبها · the long Gulf coast west of Muscat

A low, drivable shore a short run from the capital, and the water off it carries the reputation: some of the best deep-sea ground in the country. Yellowfin and sailfish over the shelf, dorado where the shelf breaks, kingfish all the way out — most of it reachable before the morning is spent.

In this water Yellowfin Tuna · Sailfish · Kingfish

See live conditions for this region →
03

Muscat

مسقط · the capital coast, Daymaniyat offshore

Inshore and offshore inside the same morning, which is why the app's own worked examples — Mutrah, Al Bustan — are both here. Kingfish and trevally over the reefs, hammour on the structure, barramundi along the shallow edges.

The Daymaniyat Islands Nature Reserve lies north-west of Al Bustan: protected, permit-controlled, and not a line to guess at. BAHHAR puts the reserve boundary on the nautical chart and flags it before you cross it — the permit itself comes from the ministry, never from the app.

In this water Kingfish · Hammour · Trevally · Barramundi

See live conditions for this region →
04

A'Sharqiyah

الشرقية · the eastern bend, from Sur out to Masirah

This is where the coast stops looking across the Gulf and starts facing the open Arabian Sea. Sur sits on that turn; Masirah, held off the shore by its own channel, is a fishing hub in its own right — grounds it does not share with the mainland.

In this water Kingfish · Yellowfin Tuna · reef fish on the shallows

See live conditions for this region →
05

Al Wusta

الوسطى · the empty central coast, Duqm and Mahoot

Sparse, and industrial rather than recreational. Four coastal wilayats — Duqm, Mahoot, Haima and Al Jazir — face the Arabian Sea with a commercial fleet behind them, so what comes ashore here is counted in crates at a landing site instead of in a day's bag.

In this water The commercial catch — pelagic and demersal, not a target list

See live conditions for this region →
06

Dhofar

ظفار · the khareef coast, Salalah to Shuwaymiyah

Oman's monsoon coast and the one with the strongest reputation: reef and inshore fishing off Salalah, then a real drop into deep water a short run south. The game season is usually counted from about mid-September, once the khareef lets go, through to the end of April — confirm the current year before you build a trip on it.

South-west of Shuwaymiyah, the Hallaniyat (Halaniyah) Islands sit in some of the least-fished, most pristine water anywhere — and they winter the world's only non-migratory humpback population. Protected and permit-controlled too; the ministry sets those terms, and they change.

In this water Snapper · Queenfish · Tuna, with giant trevally further offshore

Before you go

Sea state, permit rules and protected-area boundaries are set by Oman’s Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Water Resources, and they change. Cross-check live conditions before you leave the dock — BAHHAR informs the decision; it does not replace the ministry’s current word or your own reading of the water.
